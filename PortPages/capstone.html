2026 Capstone Project: Grit Hydroponics

Project Details and Files

Status: in progress (Capstone I). Expected completion April 2027. Last updated: [DATE]

UVU's Grit Gardens grows fresh food for Fresh Food Fridays, but production stops every October when the outdoor season ends. My capstone team is building the control system for a mobile indoor hydroponic cart that monitors and corrects its own water chemistry, so fresh food can keep coming through fall and winter.

The problem

UVU has no hydroponics unit on campus, and students have little daily contact with plants inside enclosed buildings. Grit Gardens already owns an indoor cart, but it runs on a basic timer with no sensors. If the water chemistry drifts or the tank runs dry, nobody knows until the roots die and the harvest drops.

The cart needs to:

  • Track pH, nutrient strength (EC), temperatures, humidity, and water level
  • Correct high pH automatically and sound an alarm for out-of-range readings
  • Warn before the tank runs dry
  • Stay mobile and safe around water and 120 V power

Team and my role

This is a joint project between the College of Engineering (our four-person Mechatronics capstone team) and the College of Science (a research team that determines the plant setpoints and runs the pilot grow). I am the student lead for the capstone team.

The approach

An industrial PLC runs the cart in a continuous loop: sense the water, decide whether it is in range, act (run the pump, dose acid, shut down on low water), and show everything on a screen. Water drips through two felt-pocket walls in 30-minute pump cycles and drains back to the tank, so the sensors see the whole system.

Designing for safety

The pump runs on 120 V inside a water tank, so safety shaped the design from the start: GFCI protection on the pump outlet, 24 V DC for all sensors, a hardwired E-stop that cuts every output, capped acid doses that stop if the pH sensor fails, and a low tank that resists tipping.
